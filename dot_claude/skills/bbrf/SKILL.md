# Be brief

## Code is a better spec than prose

Code is checkable, testable, and cannot drift from itself. Markdown and comments restate it and rot. Where code can hold a definition, let it; name the symbol and stop.

## Write for the reader three years out

Ask what that reader gets from a sentence. Most of what feels worth saying today is context they will already have, or noise they will have to see past.

## Your reader is at least as intelligent as you are

Give enough to work it out, not enough to follow along. Over-specifying insults the reader and buries the part they actually needed.

## Journey or state

Write what is. A journey — what it used to be, what was tried, how it got here — needs a reason to be on the page, and usually does not have one.

## Enforce what is right; do not guard what might go wrong

Say the rule. Do not spend a paragraph on a mistake nobody is likely to make; a reader who would not have made it now has to read past it.

## Say it so it does not rot

Counts, line numbers, versions, and "today" all go stale on their own. Write the sentence that stays true, or leave the fact where it maintains itself.
